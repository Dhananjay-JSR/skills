# Composition patterns

Examples use a bicycle repair shop. Translate the idea to your subject; don't copy the content.

## First-screen patterns (pick one, name it in DESIGN.md)
| Pattern | Desktop | Phone | Visual share | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Split hero** | 7/5 or 5/7 columns, type on one side, the visual on the other bleeding to the viewport edge | Visual first as a cropped band (40–50vh), headline under it | ≥40% / ≥30% | Exploded drivetrain diagram beside "Brakes bled while you wait" |
| **Type over visual** | Full-bleed illustration or photo, headline set on a calm area of it, contrast checked | Same crop, re-cropped for portrait, not just scaled | ≥60% / ≥50% | Workshop wall of hanging wheels, headline over the empty brick |
| **Artefact hero** | A real artefact with a graphic core (a job ticket with the wheel drawn on it, a parts diagram, a route map) larger than the headline, slightly rotated or overlapping the grid | Artefact cropped to the first screen, headline overlapping its edge | ≥40% / ≥30% | Repair ticket with a hand-drawn wheel and torque values |
| **Visual band** | Headline block, then a full-bleed horizontal band of visuals inside the first screen | Band scrolls horizontally (snap), with the first item visible | ≥35% / ≥30% | Strip of before/after wheel truing photos |
| **Interactive object** | A live, usable thing as the visual: a configurator, a calculator, a map, a booking slot picker with graphics | Same, thumb-sized | ≥40% / ≥30% | Tap a part on a bike drawing to see the price and time |

A card or table of text is not a visual. The visual needs shape, image, illustration or graphic at its core.

## Desktop composition
- **Asymmetric grid:** a 12-column grid used as 7/5, 8/4 or 5/7, not 6/6. Let the main visual or measure set the axis.
- **One deliberate break per screen:** an element that overlaps two sections, bleeds off the edge, or crosses the gutter. One, on purpose, consistent with the signature move.
- **Layering:** type over image, artefact over a band of color, a caption tucked into the visual. Depth comes from overlap rather than shadows everywhere.
- **Section rhythm:** vary width (full-bleed, contained, narrow measure), alignment (left, offset, split) and density. Never stack three same-shaped sections in a row.
- **Width without empty halves:** a wide screen gets side-by-side content, a sticky aside, a larger visual or a capped measure with a margin note. A centered column floating in space is not composition.
- **Scale contrast:** at least one element much larger than everything else (a headline, a number, a visual), so the eye has a start.
